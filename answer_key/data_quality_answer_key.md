## 2. How to detect each one

```python
import pandas as pd
from pathlib import Path

raw = Path("data/raw")
sales = pd.read_csv(raw / "fact_sales.csv", dtype={"txn_id": str, "customer_id": str})
cust  = pd.read_csv(raw / "dim_customer.csv", dtype={"customer_id": str})
cm    = pd.read_csv(raw / "fact_customer_month.csv", dtype={"customer_id": str})
prod  = pd.read_csv(raw / "dim_product.csv", keep_default_na=False)
```

**1 — duplicates.** `len(sales) - sales["txn_id"].nunique()` → 210. The row
count is 152,604; unique transactions are 152,394. Deduplicate on `txn_id`
before summing revenue, or you overstate it.

**2 — whitespace.** `sales["region_name"] != sales["region_name"].str.strip()` →
2,288. A `GROUP BY region_name` produces nine regions instead of five.

**3 — case.** `~cust["plan_type"].isin(["prepaid", "postpaid", "broadband"])` →
240. Values are `PREPAID` / `Prepaid` variants. A pivot on `plan_type` silently
splits the base.

**4 — null method.** `cm["payment_method"].isna().sum()` → 340. Treat as
`unknown`, never drop the row: the row still carries MRR and usage.

**5 — future signup.** `pd.to_datetime(cust["signup_date"]) > "2026-06-30"` → 9.
These customers have no `fact_customer_month` rows at all, so any "active
customers" count that starts from `dim_customer` disagrees with the snapshot
table by 9.

**6 — negative revenue.** `sales["net_revenue_usd"] < 0` → 18. They are
reversals: `discount_usd` exceeds gross. Keep them in net revenue (that is what
the ledger does), but never include them in a gross-margin or price analysis.

**7 — orphans.** `~sales["customer_id"].isin(cust["customer_id"])` → 306. They
are all `C9xxxxx` ids that do not exist in the CRM dimension. Join with
`LEFT JOIN` and count the nulls; an `INNER JOIN` loses 306 lines of revenue.

**8 — duplicate phones.** In the legacy workbook, after dropping the footer:
`phone.value_counts() > 1` → 14 phones appear twice, under different customer
ids. This is the file's only usable key, and it is not unique.

**9 — mixed dates.** Regex on the column: `^\d{2}/\d{2}/\d{4}$` matches about
half the rows, `^\d{4}-\d{2}-\d{2}$` the other half. `pd.to_datetime` with
`dayfirst=False` will silently swap day and month for the first group.

**10 — sheet structure.** `read_excel(..., skiprows=3)` and drop rows where
`Customer ID` is null or starts with `TOTAL`. A default read puts three title
rows into the data and turns the footer into a customer.

**11 — missing price date.** `prod["price_effective_from"] == ""` → 1. It is
`P01`, which has only one price, so the missing date does not change the price
for any date in the period — but a generic price-history join must decide what
to do with it rather than crash.

**12 — churn before signup.** `pd.to_datetime(cust["churn_date"]) < pd.to_datetime(cust["signup_date"])` → 4. They have zero snapshot rows. Any cohort or
lifetime calculation must exclude them explicitly.

## 3. Business impact if missed

- **Duplicates (1):** June revenue overstated by roughly 0.14%. Small enough to
  survive review and wrong enough to fail an audit.
- **Whitespace / case (2, 3):** splits five regions into nine and three plans
  into six. Every regional and plan-level share is wrong, and the totals still
  tie, so nothing looks broken.
- **Nulls (4):** `payment_method` mix percentages no longer sum to 100%; if the
  nulls are dropped, MRR is understated by 0.23%.
- **Future signups / churn before signup (5, 12):** 13 customers exist in the
  CRM dimension but never in the snapshot table. A churn rate computed from the
  dimension will not match one computed from snapshots.
- **Reversals (6):** included in revenue is correct; included in average price
  per unit is not. One reversal can move a product's average price by several
  percent in a quiet month.
- **Orphans (7):** an inner join drops 306 lines. Revenue falls, no error is
  raised, and the number is quietly wrong.
- **Legacy file (8, 9, 10):** the duplicate phones inflate the customer count by
  14; the mixed dates corrupt roughly half the signup dates; the title and
  footer rows add five fake customers and put the header in the wrong place.
- **Missing price date (11):** harmless here, fatal if the product is ever
  re-priced — the join has no key for "before the first revision".
- **Defect 2 detail:** `region_name` is denormalised and must never be used as a
  join key. `region_id` is the key.

## 4. Expected audit verdict

A competent analyst should conclude that the extract is **fit for reporting at
rep × region × product × month, conditionally**. Revenue can be summed after
deduplicating `txn_id`; targets reconcile by construction; churn and MRR can be
reported from `fact_customer_month` once the 13 impossible customers are
excluded. It is **not** fit for customer-level revenue attribution (306 orphan
lines), for price or discount analysis without excluding the 18 reversals, or
for any analysis that joins `fact_sales` to `dim_customer` without counting what
the join loses. The legacy CRM export should not be loaded at all until its
structure is fixed; it duplicates `dim_customer` and is less reliable.

Three findings must be stated in business language in the data contract:

1. We cannot attribute 306 sales lines (0.2%) to a known customer.
2. We cannot report revenue per customer from sales; use MRR instead.
3. Five regions and three plans are recorded inconsistently and must be
   normalised before any split is published.

### One judgement call worth reviewing

`units` on `P04`/`P05` is an **order quantity** (10–120 subscriptions per line),
not a subscriber count. With 12,000 customers and the given price list, a
one-subscription-per-line reading produces roughly $150k of June revenue, an
order of magnitude below the prompt's $4M–$9M acceptance band. Bulk ordering
resolves the contradiction, but it means `fact_sales` revenue and
`fact_customer_month.mrr_usd` answer different questions and must never be
reconciled to each other. If you would rather have consumer-scale revenue and
drop the $4M test, set `SUBSCRIPTION_UNITS = {"P04": (1, 1), "P05": (1, 1)}` in
the generator.
